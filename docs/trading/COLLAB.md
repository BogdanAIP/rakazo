## Why change course (Claude's review of v0–v39)

The discipline in v0–v39 is excellent: preregistration before data, SHA-pinned sources, protected holdouts, negative results kept, no orders. Keep all of that. The problems are elsewhere:

1. **Almost every test was unpowered.** One or two days, two instruments, tens of overlapping alarms. With per-trade dispersion of 60–100 bp, ~57 alarms give a standard error of ~10 bp, so a real 5 bp edge is invisible. A failure on such a sample says almost nothing, but the rule was still retired.
2. **The v38 foundation track cannot test its own hypothesis.** The memory programs need 20 earlier days; the cohort has exactly 20 days, so by construction they never act.
3. **The arena is the hardest one available.** Taker signals at 1–15 minute horizons on alt-perp L2 compete with co-located market makers. In v34 the candidate was negative before the fee, after crossing the spread alone.
4. **Reports are mostly caveats.** Limitations now live once, in this file.

## The bench

`scripts/trading/algorithm_bench_v40.py` (stdlib only), tests in `scripts/trading/test_algorithm_bench_v40.py`.

- **Typed JSON hypotheses**, never executed code. Operators: arithmetic, lag/delta/pct, rolling sum/mean/std/z/ema/tsrank, cross-sectional rank/z/demean. Each reads only bars at or before the decision bar.
- **Decision cohort** after the close of bar t: every symbol with a score, `eligible = 1` and a close at t. Nothing about later bars (entry, exit, delisting) affects who is ranked or the target weights.
- **Portfolio:** long the top quantile and short the bottom quantile (+0.5/−0.5 gross), enter at open t+1, hold `horizon_bars`. Holds longer than a day are run as **staggered tranches**: a new tranche every `rebalance_bars` (default 24 h for holds > 24 h, otherwise one tranche), each with 1/K of the gross, K = horizon / rebalance. **Each leg buys a fixed quantity at entry and holds it**: step P&L is weight × (P_end − P_start) / P_entry, funding is charged on the leg's current notional, and turnover is the change of book notional including price drift (expiring and new tranches on one contract net out). A leg whose entry bar is missing stays in cash. A leg whose boundary price is missing closes at its last observed close and pays a **300 bp penalty** on its notional; such events are counted. With one tranche the engine is identical to a plain non-overlapping backtest (tested against an independent reference).
- **Calendar:** once the first tranche trades, every step until the last trade counts, including all-cash steps (zero return), so annualization, folds and Newey–West see the real calendar.
- **Funding:** a long pays the settled rate, a short receives it. A settlement is billed in the bar ending at its nominal hour, but as a *feature* it is available only from the next decision (archive clocks lag the hour by seconds). Funding cells distinguish *no settlement* from *unknown*; unknown position-bars are counted, and above 5% of position-bars the verdict is `FUNDING_UNKNOWN`. `NA` is retrospective (it depends on later settlements), so it is used only for billing and the gate, never for features: the feature is the last observed settlement, valid for 24 hours and reset by a bar without market data.
- **Costs:** turnover × bp per side, reported at 0/5/10/15. **The gate uses 10 bp per side**: OKX Lv1 taker 5 bp plus 5 bp spread/slippage allowance for an alt basket. 5 bp is taker-only and is a sensitivity, not an all-in claim. The real account tier is unverified.
- **Development** physically truncates the panel at the frozen holdout boundary.
- **Inference:** step returns overlap when K > 1, so t-statistics, annual Sharpe and power use the Newey–West long-run variance with lag max(K − 1, 4(n/100)^(2/9)); the block bootstrap uses blocks of at least K steps. `n_effective` is reported, and fewer than 20 effective observations gives `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` (a 90-day hold on ~2.25 development years will usually land there; that is the honest answer).
- **Ledger and selection:** every development evaluation is appended with its **annual** gate Sharpe, so rules with different steps are comparable. The deflated Sharpe ratio (Bailey & López de Prado 2014) counts every hypothesis registered at freeze or evaluated on the panel, plus the audited `prior_trials`, independent of evaluation order.
- **Verdicts:** `PASS_DEV` requires DSR ≥ 0.95 at the gate cost, net mean positive in ≥ 3 of 4 chronological folds and mean IC > 0. `REJECT` only when the test had power for annual Sharpe 2 or the net mean was ≤ 0; otherwise `INCONCLUSIVE_UNPOWERED`. Also `FUNDING_UNKNOWN`, `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`.
- **Holdout:** requires a recorded `PASS_DEV`, a sign-off by a reviewer other than the author, one claim per hypothesis and at most 3 claims per panel. The sign-off is a procedural check, not a cryptographic one.
- **Scale:** arrays with NaN for missing values. A synthetic 3-year, 150-symbol hourly panel (3.5 M rows) loads in about 35 s with a peak of about 0.5 GB; one hypothesis evaluates in 5–22 s.

### Calibration (offline, reproducible from the 50 bench, builder and source-audit tests)

- Prefix invariance: for 60 random expressions, the score at bar t is byte-identical with and without later bars.
- Future delisting: deleting a symbol's rows after a decision leaves every earlier cohort and weight unchanged; the open leg is charged as a missing exit.
- Holdout leak: rewriting every holdout bar leaves every development metric identical.
- Rolling windows match a naive implementation, including at a price level of 1e5.
- Overlap: the long-run variance shrinks the effective sample of overlapping 10-step sums by the expected order of magnitude and leaves i.i.d. noise alone.
- GPT's two-step counterexample (boundary prices 100 → 120 → 132, 48 h hold, daily tranches) is a regression test: gross 0.05 then 0.055, drift-inclusive turnover 0.08 at the roll, funding on current notional.
- Long holds on 3-year synthetic noise (v40.3, 25 symbols, 20% delisting, holds 1–30 days, 48 random rules): **0 PASS_DEV** with the shared ledger and 0 without trial correction; at 0 bp, 6% of |t| exceed 1.96 against 5% expected, so the autocorrelation-robust t-statistics are calibrated. Planted funding signal: detected 2/2 at 2-day and 7-day holds for the stronger plant, 2/2 at 2 days and 0/2 at 7 days for the weaker one.
- An earlier long-hold run found 4 null passes without trial correction; all were one price-level rank repeated on one panel, plus a convexity artefact in the synthetic generator (log returns without the −σ²/2 term give volatile symbols a positive simple-return drift). The generator now has zero expected simple return. This is also why the shared ledger is mandatory: correlated variants of one lucky idea are exactly what trial correction removes.
- Pure noise with 20% of symbols delisting, 120 random hypotheses on three panels: **0 PASS_DEV** with the shared ledger and 0 without trial correction (v40.0 without delistings and with a 6 bp gate: 0 and 2).
- Planted funding signal on ~75 development days at the 10 bp gate: detected in 2/5, 3/5, 5/5 and 5/5 runs as the edge grows. Short samples miss real edges.
- On the 3-year synthetic noise panel, all 8 of Claude's hypotheses are `REJECT`: with enough data the bench gives decisive negatives instead of "unpowered".

### How much data a verdict needs

At 80% power and two-sided α = 0.05, the required history depends on the target Sharpe, not the holding horizon. Longer holds do not buy power; they only lower turnover:

| Target annual Sharpe | Development years needed |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 7.9 |
| 2 | 2.0 |
| 3 | 0.9 |

Hence about three years of data: roughly 2.25 years of development and 0.75 years of holdout.
